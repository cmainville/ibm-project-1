# IBM Project 1 - Edition B research position

**Security:** International Business Machines common stock (NYSE: IBM)  
**Decision date:** September 1, 2026 after the market close  
**Horizon:** 12-18 months, with a five-year operating forecast and terminal-value DCF  
**Decision:** **WATCH / DEFER**

The completed base case produces a DCF value of **$181.95 per diluted share**, versus a September 1 close of **$231.40**. The causal low/base/high DCF outcomes are **$144.80 / $181.95 / $223.94**. The high case remains below the market close. Oracle's EV/EBIT anchor implies $208.59 and its P/E anchor implies $185.80; Accenture's respective anchors imply $60.80 and $114.59. These are reference points rather than targets or an average because the peers differ materially in capital intensity, cloud infrastructure exposure, leverage, fiscal timing, and IBM Financing.

IBM's software growth, margin improvement and cash conversion could support future upside. The analyst should monitor software growth, consulting stabilization, infrastructure trajectory, gross margin, SG&A discipline, debt reduction and free-cash-flow conversion. The market price embeds terminal growth of **3.82%** at the base 7.07% WACC and a 15% terminal incremental ROIC, above the model's 2.5% terminal-growth assumption. The base DCF has **78.1%** terminal-value exposure, so the model is useful for framing expectations but not precise enough to justify an initiation.

Material revisions documented in Edition B: (1) FY2026 rolls from June 30 with H2 forecast only, rather than double-counting H1 in cash and equity; (2) the post-close stub is 121/184 of H2 FCFF, not 122/184; (3) the September 10 dividend is a single payable in the bridge, because it was declared before the valuation date and remained unpaid; (4) IBM Financing is excluded from operating FCFF and added once as the June 30 reported $1.45bn equity sidecar; and (5) the P/E peer application now uses FY2026E consolidated net income as a labeled common-earnings proxy, not NOPAT.

The recommendation would improve only after evidence supports a value above price under a conservative case. Reversal triggers include sustained software revenue growth below the base path, gross-margin or SG&A deterioration, weaker cash conversion, material net-debt increase, or validation that pension/other claims reduce equity value. Positive review triggers include two reported quarters supporting the operating path plus reproducible beta, IBM-specific cost-of-debt, pension and point-in-time diluted-share evidence.

All monetary data are USD millions except per-share values. Forecasts are analyst estimates; reported facts and calculation outputs are distinguished in `inputs.json`, the source ledger, and the workbench.
