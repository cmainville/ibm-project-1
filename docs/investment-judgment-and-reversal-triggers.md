# IBM investment judgment and reversal triggers

## Committee action: Watch / Defer

IBM has potentially attractive software-led opportunities, recurring enterprise
relationships, and a base operating valuation that is internally articulated.
Those positives do not make the September 1, 2026 price of **$231.40/share** a
compelling entry. The approved DCF cases are **$145.13 / $182.29 / $224.28** per
share: low is $86.27 (37.3%) below market, base is $49.11 (21.2%) below, and
the named high causal case is still $7.12 (3.1%) below. The appropriate present
action is Watch / Defer, not a forced initiation or a claim that the stock is
uninvestable at every future price.

## What the market requires

At the 7.0708% WACC and 15% terminal incremental ROIC, reverse DCF solves for
**3.8150%** terminal growth to reach $231.40—above the approved 2.5% terminal
growth and outside the workbench’s 2–3% documented range. Terminal PV is
**78.06%** of operating EV, so this is an economically consequential
expectation rather than a rounding issue.

The current WACC/growth grid crosses market only under comparatively favorable
discount-rate/growth pairs: 6.57% WACC with 3.0% growth produces $229.71
(still below), while 6.07% WACC with 2.5% growth produces $243.39 and 6.07%
with 3.0% produces $272.66. At base WACC, even 3.0% growth yields $197.29.
Those crossings require either a lower WACC than the approved base or terminal
growth above the base, and they do not independently resolve cash conversion,
future dilution, or bridge uncertainty. A price-crossing scenario alone is not
a sufficient margin of safety.

The OAT ranking depends on different shock sizes: gross margin receives a 1pp
shock, Software a 2pp shock, and SG&A a 0.5pp shock. It therefore identifies
model response to documented shocks, not a standardized claim that one business
driver is universally more important than another. Operating, discount-rate,
terminal, and bridge sensitivities are not combined because doing so would
imply an unsupported joint scenario.

## Principal vulnerabilities and evidence needed to re-underwrite

1. **Cash conversion — highest priority.** The FY2026 company-style FCF proxy
   is $10.918bn versus an approximate $15.7bn management-outlook reference.
   Obtain the reported H2/FY CFO, Financing-receivable movement, cash-tax
   detail, net capital investment and asset-sale bridge. A credible, disclosed
   bridge that explains the variance would improve confidence; a repeat cash
   shortfall would weaken the operating case.
2. **Terminal and WACC inputs.** A cutoff-valid reproducible beta regression
   and IBM-specific dated borrowing-cost evidence could justify revisiting the
   discount rate. They should not be backfilled from later market data.
3. **Software durability and margins.** Evidence of sustained software growth,
   renewal/pricing strength, and durable gross-margin expansion could support
   higher operating forecasts. Evidence of AI-related substitution, competitive
   pricing pressure, or weak Consulting execution would argue the other way.
4. **Enterprise-to-equity bridge.** Reconcile point-in-time diluted shares and
   future SBC-related dilution, IBM Financing fair value, and pension claims
   before treating a small apparent upside as investable.

## Reversal conditions

**Consider initiation only if:** updated cutoff-consistent evidence improves
cash conversion and operating durability, the revised valuation establishes a
meaningful margin above the market price after conservative bridge/dilution
treatment, and WACC/terminal inputs have stronger support.

**Continue monitoring if:** software and margins improve but H2/FY cash
conversion or bridge evidence remains unresolved; favorable growth alone does
not cure the $4.78bn FCF qualification.

**Strengthen a negative view if:** disclosed cash conversion remains materially
below management’s earlier outlook without a credible definition bridge,
Software/Consulting performance weakens, capex or tax demands rise, or
dilution/Financing/pension claims erode the equity bridge.

This is a decision framework, not a new forecast, probability weighting, or
price target.
